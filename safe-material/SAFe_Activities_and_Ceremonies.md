# SAFe Activities and Ceremonies

Extracted from source material in this folder.

---

## Portfolio Level

| Activity | Description |
|---|---|
| **Strategic Portfolio Review** | The Strategic Portfolio Review is an LPM event focused on achieving and advancing the Portfolio Vision by providing continuous strategy, implementation, and budget alignment. It is typically held on a quarterly cadence, at least one month before the next PI Planning event. The outcome is updated Strategic Themes, Portfolio Vision, Portfolio Roadmap, and approved and prioritized Epics aligned to the portfolio strategy. |
| **Portfolio Sync** | The Portfolio Sync is an LPM event focused on portfolio operations that provides visibility into how well the portfolio is progressing toward its strategic objectives. It is typically held weekly, every other week, or monthly depending on portfolio maturity. The outcome is reviewed Epic and Value Stream execution status, resolved cross-product dependencies, escalated and removed impediments, and an updated Portfolio Roadmap. |
| **Participatory Budgeting** | Participatory Budgeting is an LPM event focused on establishing and adjusting Lean Budgets across Development Value Streams in a collaborative, decentralized manner. It is typically held every two PIs, involving stakeholders from different Value Streams organized into small groups to fund baseline solution investments and proposed solution initiatives. The outcome is adjusted Value Stream budget allocations that reflect stakeholder consensus and alignment with portfolio Guardrails and Strategic Themes. |
| **SAFe Value Stream and ART Identification Workshop** | The SAFe Value Stream and ART Identification Workshop is a facilitated workshop led by an experienced SAFe Program Consultant (SPC) to help an organization identify and structure its Development Value Streams and Agile Release Trains. It is run periodically to optimize the flow of value within a portfolio, particularly during initial SAFe adoption or organizational restructuring. The outcome is a clear mapping of Development Value Streams and ARTs that aligns the organizational structure around value delivery rather than functional silos. |
| **Lean Portfolio Management Assessment** | The LPM Assessment is a structured evaluation tool that enables the Enterprise to assess its proficiency across the three LPM collaborations: Strategy and Investment Funding, Agile Portfolio Operations, and Lean Governance. It is conducted periodically, typically as part of ongoing improvement cycles or SAFe implementation checkpoints. The outcome is an identified set of strengths and improvement areas in LPM competency that guides the organization's continuous improvement efforts toward Business Agility. |
| **SAFe Business Agility Assessment** | The SAFe Business Agility Assessment is an enterprise-wide evaluation tool that measures overall progress across all seven SAFe Core Competencies of Business Agility. It is conducted periodically to provide a holistic view of the organization's Lean-Agile maturity. The outcome is a radar chart showing competency proficiency scores that helps leadership identify priority areas for improvement across the entire enterprise. |
| **Measure and Grow** | Measure and Grow is a SAFe practice in which the organization uses assessments and Metrics to evaluate performance across teams, ARTs, and the portfolio in order to drive relentless improvement. It occurs on a continuous basis, with formal assessments run periodically throughout the SAFe transformation journey. The outcome is objective data on organizational performance that informs improvement actions, coaching priorities, and investment decisions to advance Business Agility. |
| **LACE Formation Workshop** | The LACE Formation Workshop is a structured event used to establish the Lean-Agile Center of Excellence, defining its purpose, operating model, and initial backlog of work. It is run once during the initial stages of a SAFe adoption or when restructuring the LACE. The outcome is a formed LACE team with a clear mission, agreed ways of working, and an initial plan for supporting SAFe implementation across the enterprise. |
| **Portfolio Canvas Workshop** | The Portfolio Canvas Workshop is a collaborative activity in which LPM stakeholders define the current and future state of the portfolio by completing the Portfolio Canvas, capturing Value Streams, Solutions, Customer Segments, Key Activities, and financial information. It is conducted during portfolio strategy sessions, typically as part of establishing or revisiting the Portfolio Vision. The outcome is a completed current-state and future-state Portfolio Canvas that provides a shared understanding of the portfolio's business model and informs strategic decisions. |
| **SWOT Analysis** | The SWOT Analysis is a portfolio strategy activity in which LPM evaluates the portfolio's internal Strengths and Weaknesses alongside external Opportunities and Threats. It is conducted as part of the portfolio strategy formulation process, typically during Strategic Portfolio Reviews or Vision-setting sessions. The outcome is a structured understanding of the portfolio's current competitive position that serves as input to the TOWS analysis and the development of strategic options. |
| **TOWS Analysis** | The TOWS Analysis is a portfolio strategy activity that builds on the SWOT Analysis by generating strategic options that leverage strengths to exploit opportunities, overcome threats, address weaknesses, and minimize risks. It is conducted after the SWOT Analysis as part of the portfolio future-state planning process. The outcome is a set of strategic options and initiatives, often expressed as Epics or Enablers, that guide the evolution of the portfolio toward its future-state Portfolio Canvas and Vision. |
| **Epic Hypothesis Statement Definition** | Epic Hypothesis Statement Definition is the practice of formally describing a portfolio Epic using a structured template that captures the value statement, business outcome hypothesis, leading indicators, and nonfunctional requirements. It occurs during the Reviewing stage of the Portfolio Kanban when an Epic is pulled from the Funnel and refined by an Epic Owner and key stakeholders. The outcome is a clearly articulated Epic Hypothesis Statement that provides the foundation for analysis, Lean business case development, and LPM approval decisions. |
| **Lean Business Case Analysis** | Lean Business Case Analysis is the activity conducted during the Analyzing stage of the Portfolio Kanban in which an Epic Owner assembles stakeholders to evaluate solution alternatives, define the MVP, estimate costs, and prepare a go/no-go recommendation for LPM. It occurs when an Epic is pulled into the Analyzing state, subject to WIP limits. The outcome is a completed Lean Business Case with MVP scope, forecasted costs, incremental implementation strategy, and a go/no-go decision that determines whether the Epic progresses to the Portfolio Backlog. |
| **MVP Build and Evaluation** | MVP Build and Evaluation is the practice of implementing and assessing the Minimum Viable Product for an approved Epic using the SAFe Lean Startup Cycle to validate the business outcome hypothesis. It occurs during the Implementing stage of the Portfolio Kanban after the Lean Business Case has been approved by LPM. The outcome is validated learning about whether the Epic hypothesis is proven true or false, leading to a pivot, persevere, or stop decision that guides subsequent portfolio investment. |
| **WSJF Prioritization** | WSJF Prioritization is the practice of sequencing Epics in the Portfolio Backlog using the Weighted Shortest Job First formula, which divides Cost of Delay by normalized Epic cost to determine the economic priority order for implementation. It occurs during the Ready and Analyzing stages of the Portfolio Kanban, as part of portfolio sync and strategic portfolio review events. The outcome is a rank-ordered Portfolio Backlog that ensures ARTs pull the highest-value, shortest-duration Epics first, maximizing the economic return on portfolio investments. |

## Large Solution (Solution Train) Level

| Activity | Description |
|---|---|
| **Solution Demo** | The Solution Demo is a Large Solution-level event in which the combined output of all ARTs and Suppliers contributing to a Solution is demonstrated to key stakeholders. It occurs at the end of each PI or on a cadence defined by the Solution Train. The outcome is an integrated demonstration of the full Solution that provides objective evidence of progress and enables informed decision-making at the portfolio and enterprise level. |

## ART Level

| Activity | Description |
|---|---|
| **PI Planning** | PI Planning is a cadence-based, face-to-face event that aligns all teams on an Agile Release Train to a shared mission and Vision for an upcoming Program Increment. It occurs once per PI, typically every 8–12 weeks, and is a foundational planning ceremony in SAFe. The outcome is a set of PI Objectives and ART plans that commit teams to delivering value while identifying cross-team dependencies and risks. |
| **Inspect and Adapt** | Inspect and Adapt is a significant ART event held at the end of each Program Increment in which the ART demonstrates its work and reflects on how to improve. It occurs at the conclusion of every PI and includes a PI System Demo, quantitative measurement, and a structured problem-solving workshop. The outcome is a set of improvement stories added to the ART backlog to drive relentless improvement in team and ART performance. |
| **PI System Demo** | The PI System Demo is an event in which the Agile Release Train demonstrates all Features completed during the Program Increment to Business Owners and other stakeholders. It occurs at the end of each PI, serving as an objective milestone based on evaluation of working systems. The outcome is validated evidence of solution feasibility, stakeholder feedback, and quantitative data on PI Objectives achievement used to inform portfolio-level decisions. |
| **System Demo** | The System Demo is a regular ART-level event in which all teams integrate and demonstrate their combined work to stakeholders at the end of each Iteration. It occurs every Iteration, typically every two weeks, providing a consistent integration point across the ART. The outcome is fast feedback on the integrated solution, enabling early detection of issues and continuous validation of the solution's progress toward PI Objectives. |
| **ART Sync** | The ART Sync is an ART-level ceremony in which Release Train Engineers, Scrum Masters, and team representatives coordinate cross-team dependencies, impediments, and progress toward PI Objectives. It occurs on a regular cadence during the PI, typically weekly or biweekly. The outcome is visibility into ART-level progress, resolved cross-team blockers, and updated program-level plans. |
| **Scrum of Scrums** | The Scrum of Scrums is an ART-level coordination ceremony in which representatives from each Agile Team meet to discuss integration points, dependencies, and impediments. It occurs regularly during the PI, typically weekly, as part of the ART Sync or as a standalone event. The outcome is improved cross-team coordination, escalated and resolved impediments, and alignment on shared ART-level commitments. |
| **Product Owner Sync** | The Product Owner Sync is an ART-level ceremony in which Product Owners align with Product Management on priorities, scope, and upcoming work. It occurs on a regular cadence during the PI, typically weekly or biweekly. The outcome is synchronized priorities across teams, clarified backlog items, and alignment between team-level and ART-level objectives. |
| **Communities of Practice** | Communities of Practice are informal groups of practitioners who share a common interest in a specific area, such as Scrum Masters, Technical Coaches, or Release Train Engineers, and who collaborate to improve their skills and share knowledge. They operate on a self-organized, ongoing basis without a fixed schedule mandated by SAFe. The outcome is improved practitioner capability, harvested best practices, and stronger cross-team and cross-ART collaboration within the enterprise. |
| **PI System Demo (PI Milestone)** | The PI System Demo milestone is an objective evaluation point at the end of each Program Increment that demonstrates all Features completed during the PI to Business Owners and stakeholders. It occurs once per PI and serves as a principle-based milestone grounded in objective evaluation of working systems rather than task completion or phase-gate criteria. The outcome is measured PI Objective achievement scores, stakeholder feedback, and validated progress toward the Portfolio Vision that informs the next PI's planning. |

## Team Level

| Activity | Description |
|---|---|
| **Iteration Planning** | Iteration Planning is a team-level ceremony in which the Agile Team plans the work to be completed during the upcoming Iteration. It occurs at the start of every Iteration, typically every two weeks, and involves selecting backlog items and defining team-level iteration goals. The outcome is a committed Iteration plan with defined Stories, tasks, and an Iteration goal that aligns with the current PI Objectives. |
| **Iteration Review** | The Iteration Review is a team-level ceremony held at the end of each Iteration in which the team demonstrates the working software or system increment completed during the Iteration. It occurs every Iteration and provides stakeholders with visibility into the team's progress. The outcome is accepted Stories, stakeholder feedback, and updated team backlogs that reflect any new insights or changes in priority. |
| **Iteration Retrospective** | The Iteration Retrospective is a team-level ceremony in which the Agile Team reflects on its processes, practices, and teamwork to identify improvements. It occurs at the end of every Iteration, after the Iteration Review. The outcome is a short list of actionable improvement items that the team commits to implementing in the next Iteration. |
| **Daily Stand-up** | The Daily Stand-up is a brief daily ceremony in which team members synchronize on progress, plan for the next 24 hours, and surface any impediments. It occurs every working day, typically lasting no more than 15 minutes. The outcome is a shared understanding of daily progress toward Iteration goals and identification of blockers that need to be resolved. |
| **Backlog Refinement** | Backlog Refinement is a team-level practice in which the Agile Team reviews, estimates, and prepares backlog items for upcoming Iterations, including the decomposition of Epics into Features and Enablers prior to PI Planning. It occurs continuously throughout the Iteration on a cadence determined by the team. The outcome is a refined and estimated backlog of Stories and Enablers that are ready for planning and implementation. |
